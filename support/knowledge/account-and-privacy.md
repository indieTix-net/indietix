# Account, privacy and status

## The venue's web address
Each venue can pick its own address, like yourvenue.indietix.net, in Client Settings or at signup. Its login page fills in the Client ID, and its terms and privacy pages show the venue's own details (entered under Client Settings → Terms & Privacy Pages).

## Privacy requests from ticket buyers
- When someone asks a venue what it holds about them, or asks to be erased, the venue's main login uses the **Privacy Requests** page (linked from Client Settings).
- It finds their orders, tickets and records by email or phone, can download everything as a file, and can erase their name, email and phone everywhere. Sales and reports stay correct, and their tickets keep working.
- The venue should confirm the request really came from that person first, for example by replying to the email address on their order.
- A ticket buyer with a privacy request should contact the venue that sold the ticket. Privacy questions about IndieTix itself are answered personally by IndieTix.

## Closing an account
Email support from the venue's contact address. IndieTix confirms, then deletes the venue's data. Removing IndieTix's access in Square stops new orders reaching IndieTix straight away.

## Is something down?
The status page (status.html on the venue's site) shows whether ticket sales, the box office pages and door scanning, text messages, nightly reports and marketing posts are working. IndieTix is alerted automatically when something fails.

## Reporting a security problem
Please don't put details in an email to support. Use GitHub's private "Report a vulnerability" on https://github.com/indieTix-net/indietix (Security tab), or email indietix@indietix.net.
